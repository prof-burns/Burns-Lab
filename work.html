Our Work

Research

The Human Microbiome

The microbial communities of the gut and skin are in constant exchange with host tissues, and we study how that relationship shifts with diet, drug exposure, and disease. Our clinical studies, run with dermatologists and their patients, focus on inflammatory skin disorders such as erythroderma and epidermolysis bullosa. In these cohorts we pair microbiome sequencing with host proteomics and spatial transcriptomics, and we test treatments aimed at restoring a healthier skin community.

Cancer Metagenomics

Tumors and the tissues around them carry their own microbial communities, and we use metagenomic sequencing and bioinformatics to ask how those communities relate to cancer development, treatment, and outcomes. Our largest current effort is in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, where we have characterized skin, nasal, and gut dysbiosis, followed how the microbiome responds to phototherapy and radiation, and documented racial disparities in infection risk and skin microbiota. We also study breast and colorectal cancers, including how tumor mutations track with the microbes in the tumor microenvironment.

Environmental Metagenomics

We bring the same sequencing and analysis tools to microbial communities outside the clinic, usually in partnership with an ecologist. Recent projects include the spotted turtle gut microbiome (Joseph Milanovich), ancient DNA in lake sediment cores (Yoel Stuart), and seasonal changes in Lake St. Benedict (Leigh Anne Harden, Benedictine University). Earlier papers looked at gut microbiomes in wild primates and in BaAka and Bantu communities.

Collaborate With Us

If you're planning a metagenomics study or have -omic data you're stuck on, get in touch. It's best to talk before you collect samples, while sampling, controls, and sequencing depth are still easy to change. Email mburns16@luc.edu with a short description of your system and your question.

Programs and Outreach

AI and Human Flourishing

Dr. Burns directs Loyola's Artificial Intelligence and Human Flourishing minor, which Computer Science and Philosophy launched in fall 2026. Students learn how AI systems work and how to judge where they help and where they harm. He also leads faculty workshops on teaching with generative AI.

Character Education

Dr. Burns and his co-PIs, Joseph Vukov (Philosophy), Shannon Howes (Student Development), and Jim Flavin (Student Development), received a three-year Institutional Impact Grant (2026 to 2029) from the Educating Character Initiative, funded by Lilly Endowment. The project works with faculty and undergraduate students to bring character education into their experiences across Loyola, from the classroom to student life. Each year we recruit undergraduate ambassadors and faculty fellows to work with us on the project, so reach out if you're interested.

Program Assessment

Dr. Burns has been an Assessment Faculty Fellow in the Provost's Office since 2023. He works with programs across Loyola on annual assessment reports and academic program reviews. He's also available as an external reviewer for biology, bioinformatics, and interdisciplinary science programs.

Science Communication

Dr. Burns and Joseph Vukov (Philosophy) give public talks on science, ethics, and religion. Recent topics include human genetic engineering, de-extinction, and engineering humans for Mars. They also run faculty sessions on co-teaching across disciplines.

Community Outreach

We've worked with a large number of community groups to design programming at the intersections of science, ethics, religion, and technology. Partners have included Interfaith America, the Little Sisters of the Poor, GirlForward, EvolVR, and many others. Dr. Burns has also mentored in Interfaith America's faculty network since 2017.
